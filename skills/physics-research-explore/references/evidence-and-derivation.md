# Evidence, derivation, and reproduction

## Literature evidence

Map more than search hits: identify the standard approach, competing approaches, accepted open problem, and this project's position. For each claim that matters:

1. Record the source and stable identifier.
2. State what part was actually inspected.
3. State the claim and the assumptions under which the source supports it.
4. Mark inaccessible or paywalled material as unverified rather than guessing.

Prefer primary papers and official records. A resolvable identifier verifies existence, not support; support requires inspecting the relevant content.

## Derivations

Build in dependency order and state assumptions when used. Mark important equations as one of:

- derived here and independently checked;
- taken from a verified source, with location;
- checked symbolically or numerically, with method;
- unchecked.

Use dimensional analysis, symmetries, signs, limiting cases, normalization, and factors such as `2π` and `ℏ` as independent checks. If a symbolic or numerical engine is available, verify its availability and version at runtime; never assume a particular tool exists.

## Reproduction

Record the paper, equation or figure, conventions, and source assumptions. Explain any divergence in method. When results disagree, test convention changes, source errata, differing assumptions, and mistakes on both sides. Preserve the disagreement until evidence resolves it.

## Explanation quality

Define before using and motivate before formalizing. After a substantial explanation, use one or two targeted checks of understanding when interaction is useful; avoid a generic “does that make sense?”. Change the route or level if the explanation did not land.
